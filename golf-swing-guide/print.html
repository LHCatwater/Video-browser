SetupStep 2 of 31

Pick up a 7-iron

Learn the whole swing with one club: a 7-iron.

  • It is the middle of the bag: not too long, not too short.
  • It is forgiving and gets the ball in the air easily.
  • Everything you learn with it carries over to the other clubs.
Feel
Same club every session until step 21.
Check
The number 7 is stamped on the bottom of the club head.
Practise
Just hold it and get used to the weight. Swing it gently back and forth like a pendulum.

SetupStep 3 of 31

Left hand on the grip

Lay the grip across the base of your left fingers, not in your palm.

  • Hold the club out in front of you with your right hand, club head on the ground.
  • Place the grip diagonally from the base of your little finger to the middle of your index finger.
  • Close your fingers, then close the hand so the left thumb sits just right of the centre of the grip.
Feel
The club is held by your fingers. The palm just closes over it.
Check
Look down: you can see 2 knuckles on the back of your left hand.
Practise
Hold the club out horizontally with only the left hand. If you can support it with your fingers and the pad of your hand, the grip is right.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

SetupStep 4 of 31

Right hand on the grip

Add your right hand so its palm faces the target and covers your left thumb.

  • Put the grip in the fingers of your right hand, just below the left hand.
  • Fold the right palm over so it covers your left thumb.
  • Link the hands: hook the right little finger between the left index and middle fingers (interlock), or simply keep all ten fingers on the grip. Either is fine. Pick one and keep it.
Feel
The two hands feel like one unit, not two separate hands.
Check
The V shapes made by each thumb and index finger both point at your right shoulder.
Practise
Take your grip, let go, and re-grip 10 times in a row. Check the V shapes each time.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

SetupStep 5 of 31

Grip pressure: 4 out of 10

Hold the club firmly enough to control it, loosely enough to stay relaxed.

  • On a scale where 1 is barely holding and 10 is squeezing as hard as you can, aim for about 4.
  • Your forearms should feel soft, not tense.
  • Beginners almost always squeeze too hard. Lighter is usually better.
Feel
Like holding a tube of toothpaste without squeezing any out.
Check
Someone could pull the club out of your hands with a firm tug.
Practise
Squeeze to 10, then relax to 1, then settle at 4. Do this before each practice swing.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

SetupStep 6 of 31

Aim the clubface first

Point the clubface at your target before you set your feet.

  • Stand behind the ball and pick a target.
  • Find a small spot on the ground (a leaf, a mark) about a foot in front of the ball, on the line to the target.
  • Walk in and set the bottom edge of the clubface square to that spot.
Feel
Aim at the close spot, not the far target. It is much easier.
Check
The bottom edge (leading edge) of the club looks like a line at right angles to your target line.
Practise
Lay a club or alignment stick on the ground pointing at the target. Practise setting the clubface square to it.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

SetupStep 7 of 31

Line up your body

Set your feet, hips and shoulders parallel to the target line.

  • Think of railway tracks: the ball and clubface are on the outer rail, pointing at the target.
  • Your toes are on the inner rail.
  • Because of this, your body points slightly left of the target. That is correct.
Feel
Body aims parallel left, clubface aims at the target.
Check
Lay a club across your toes. It should be parallel to the target line, not pointing at the target.
Practise
Use two clubs on the ground: one along the target line, one along your toes. Practise stepping in to them.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

SetupStep 8 of 31

Stance width

Stand with your feet about shoulder width apart.

  • Measure from the insides of your heels to the outside of your shoulders.
  • Point your feet straight ahead, or turn the left foot out slightly.
  • Too narrow and you lose balance. Too wide and you can't turn.
Feel
Stable and athletic, like you're ready to catch a ball.
Check
Hold a club across your shoulders and let it drop down: it should land near the insides of your feet.
Practise
Take your stance, then have a gentle nudge test: you shouldn't wobble if pushed lightly from the side.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

SetupStep 9 of 31

Ball in the middle

For a 7-iron, put the ball in the middle of your stance.

  • Halfway between your two feet.
  • If anything, a fraction to the left of centre is fine.
  • Later, shorter clubs go slightly back and longer clubs go further forward. Don't worry about that yet.
Feel
The ball sits under the logo on your shirt.
Check
Lay a club on the ground from the ball straight back toward you. It should point between your feet.
Practise
Put a tee in the ground at the middle of your stance. Step in, check, step away, repeat.

SetupStep 10 of 31

Bend from the hips

Tilt forward by pushing your hips back, keeping your back straight.

  • Stand tall with the club across the front of your hips.
  • Push the club back into the crease of your hips, and tilt your chest forward over the ball.
  • Keep your back straight, not hunched or rounded.
Feel
Like closing a car door with your bottom while your hands are full.
Check
From the side, your back is a straight line and your bottom sticks out behind your heels.
Practise
Do this tilt 10 times in front of a mirror or window. Check your back is straight each time.

SetupStep 11 of 31

Soft knees, hanging arms

Flex your knees slightly and let your arms hang straight down.

  • After the hip tilt, unlock your knees a little. Not a squat.
  • Let your arms hang naturally from your shoulders.
  • Your hands should be roughly under your chin, about a fist's width from your thighs.
Feel
Relaxed, like a tennis player waiting for a serve.
Check
A hand's width (a fist) fits between the end of the grip and your thighs.
Practise
Take your setup, let go of the club with one hand and check the fist gap. Then re-grip.

SetupStep 12 of 31

Balance your weight

Spread your weight evenly between both feet, over the middle of your feet.

  • Not on your heels, not on your toes.
  • About 50/50 between left and right foot.
  • You should feel ready to move, like a goalkeeper.
Feel
Weight over the laces of your shoes.
Check
You could lift your toes slightly without falling backwards, and lift your heels slightly without falling forwards.
Practise
Rock gently heels-to-toes, then settle in the middle. Do this before every shot for a week.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

The swingStep 13 of 31

The takeaway

Start the swing by moving the club, hands, arms and chest together, low and slow.

  • Turn your chest away and let the club go back with it, all as one piece.
  • Keep the club head low to the ground for the first foot or so.
  • Stop when your hands reach hip height.
Feel
A triangle made by your arms and shoulders turns away together.
Check
At hip height, the club is parallel to the ground and the toe of the club points up to the sky.
Practise
Take the club back to hip height and stop. Check the toe points up. Return. 20 times.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

The swingStep 14 of 31

Hinge the wrists

As your hands pass hip height, hinge your wrists so the club points up.

  • Keep turning, and let your wrists cock the club upward.
  • By the time your left arm is parallel to the ground, the club should point up at the sky.
  • This forms an L shape between your left arm and the club.
Feel
Your thumbs move up toward your right shoulder.
Check
Left arm parallel to the ground, club pointing up: an L shape.
Practise
Swing back to the L position and hold for 2 seconds. Check in a mirror or window reflection.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

The swingStep 15 of 31

Turn to the top

Keep turning until your back faces the target.

  • Your shoulders turn about a quarter turn (90 degrees).
  • Your hips turn too, but less.
  • Your weight moves into your right foot, on the inside of the foot. Don't let your body slide to the right.
Feel
A wind-up, like a spring being coiled.
Check
At the top, your left shoulder is under your chin and your left arm is fairly straight (not locked).
Practise
Hold a club across your chest, take your setup and turn until the club points at the ball. Repeat 10 times.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

The swingStep 16 of 31

Start down with your feet

Begin the downswing by shifting your weight to your left foot.

  • Before your arms do anything, push your weight across to your left side.
  • Your left hip moves slightly toward the target.
  • Your arms will start falling naturally. Don't throw them at the ball.
Feel
Like stepping into a throw. The lower body leads, the arms follow.
Check
Halfway down, more of your weight is on your left foot than your right.
Practise
Step-through drill: start with feet together, swing back, step your left foot toward the target, then swing through.

The swingStep 17 of 31

Turn through

Keep turning your hips and chest toward the target as you swing down.

  • Once your weight has shifted, rotate.
  • Your belt buckle turns toward the target.
  • Your arms come along for the ride. Rotation powers the swing, not your arms.
Feel
Your belly button chases the target.
Check
As the club reaches the ball, your hips have already started to open toward the target.
Practise
Half swings (hip height to hip height) focusing only on turning your belt buckle to the target.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

The swingStep 18 of 31

Ball first, then ground

Hit the ball first, then the ground, with your hands slightly ahead of the ball.

  • With an iron you hit down on the ball. The club's angle lifts it into the air. Don't try to scoop it.
  • Your hands are slightly closer to the target than the club head at impact.
  • Any grass you take (a divot) should be in front of where the ball was.
Feel
Trap the ball between the clubface and the ground.
Check
Your divot, or brush mark, starts on the target side of the ball.
Practise
Draw a line on the ground (or lay a towel) and put the ball on it. Try to brush the ground just past the line.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

The swingStep 19 of 31

Extend toward the target

After impact, let both arms stretch out toward the target.

  • Keep swinging through the ball, not at it.
  • Both arms straighten after the ball has gone.
  • Your forearms roll over naturally. Don't stop or hold them back.
Feel
Reaching out to shake hands with someone standing on the target line.
Check
Just after impact, both arms are straight and pointing down the target line.
Practise
Half swings, freezing the position just after impact. Check both arms are extended.

The swingStep 20 of 31

Hold your finish

Finish facing the target, balanced on your left foot.

  • Your chest and belt buckle face the target.
  • Nearly all your weight (about 90%) is on your left foot.
  • Your right heel is up, resting on the toe of your right shoe. The club is over your left shoulder.
Feel
Pose for the photo.
Check
You can hold the finish without wobbling for 3 seconds.
Practise
Hit every practice ball and hold the finish for a count of three. If you can't, swing slower.

Put it togetherStep 21 of 31

Smooth rhythm

Swing at about 75% effort with a smooth, even rhythm.

  • The backswing is slower than the downswing.
  • Say “one-and” on the way back and “two” as you swing through.
  • Smooth beats hard. Swinging harder usually makes the ball go less far.
Feel
Unhurried at the top. No jerk from backswing to downswing.
Check
Your finish (step 19) is balanced. If it isn't, your rhythm is too fast.
Practise
Hit 10 balls saying “one-and… two” out loud. Same rhythm every time.
Practice log: at least 10 reps per session. Tick the step once you have 2 sessions and the latest has 7 out of 10 passing the Check.

A pre-shot routine

Do the same short routine before every shot.

  • Stand behind the ball and pick a target.
  • Find your spot a foot in front of the ball (step 6).
  • Set the clubface, then your feet. One practice waggle, one breath, then swing.
Feel
Same steps, same order, every time.
Check
The routine takes about the same time on every shot, roughly 15 to 20 seconds.
Practise
For a whole range session, never hit a ball without doing the full routine first.

Driver: tee it high

Tee the ball so half of it sits above the top of the driver.

  • Only start on the driver once your 7-iron swing feels comfortable.

Driver changesStep 27 of 31

Driver: sweep it

Swing level or slightly upward through the ball. No divot.

  • The same swing as your irons. Only the setup has changed.
  • Because the ball is forward and you are tilted, the club catches it on the way up.
  • Don't try to lift it. Trust the setup.
Feel
Sweep the ball off the tee like brushing crumbs off a table.
Check
The tee often stays in the ground or flies just forward. You don't hit the turf.
Practise
Tee a ball and place a second tee 30cm in front of it. Try to clip that second tee after hitting the ball.

Quick fixesStep 28 of 31

Hitting the ground first

If you hit the ground before the ball (a fat shot), shift your weight to your left foot earlier.

  • Fat shots usually mean your weight stayed on your right foot.
  • Go back to step 15 and start the downswing with your feet.
  • Check the ball isn't too far forward (step 9).
Feel
Lean into your left side as you swing down.
Check
Your divot starts after where the ball was.
Practise
Hit balls with your right heel lifted off the ground. It forces your weight onto your left foot.

Quick fixesStep 29 of 31

Topping the ball

If you hit the top of the ball, keep your chest down until after impact.

  • Topping usually means you stood up too early, trying to see where the ball went.
  • Keep the bend from step 10 all the way through the ball.
  • Let the finish lift you up, not the downswing.
Feel
Watch the spot where the ball was for a moment after you hit it.
Check
After impact you can still see the ground where the ball sat.
Practise
Place a coin behind the ball and keep looking at it until the ball has gone.

Quick fixesStep 30 of 31

Ball curving right

If the ball curves away to the right (a slice), check your grip first.

  • A slice means the clubface is open (pointing right) at impact.
  • Go back to step 3: make sure you can see 2 knuckles on your left hand.
  • Then step 18: let your forearms roll over after impact.
Feel
Your right hand rolls over the left after the ball.
Check
Both thumb V shapes point at your right shoulder.
Practise
Hit half swings feeling the toe of the club point up again on the follow-through, mirroring step 12.
